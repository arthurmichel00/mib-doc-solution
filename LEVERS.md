# LEVERS — what was built, what shipped, and why the rest did not

Every lever attempted during the build, with its verdict and the measurement that produced it. Derived from
the project's append-only approach ledger, which existed so no loop iteration would re-litigate a settled
dead end. Scores are on the official evaluator: train /150 over 1,000 cases, holdout = the fixed 200-case
seed-8090 subset. Referenced from MEMO.md and APPENDIX.md.

**Verdict keys.** `SHIPPED-GATED` in the tree behind a flag or decision · `SHIPPED-DORMANT` in the tree,
default off · `REJECTED-ON-SAFETY` works, but harms clean cases or cannot be gated · `NO-GO-ON-VALUE`
measured net-negative or net-zero · `REJECTED-ON-MEASUREMENT` works in isolation, loses points in
composition · `KILLED-ON-EVIDENCE` built, then killed by its own targeted census · `DEAD-END` confirmed no
headroom · `VERIFIED-SAFE` audited, no change needed · `PENDING-A/B` built and green, first corpus
measurement not yet run · `DEFERRED` post-submission queue. **Seventeen levers ship enabled** (the rows in
"Shipped" below); that configuration is also enumerated in MEMO.md and SUBMISSION.md.

## Standing conclusions

- **The binding constraint is reading power on the hard tail**, not orientation plumbing, not adjudication
  logic, and not calibration. Six independent lines of evidence converge on it: escalation net-negative,
  template adjudication 1/19, discharge heads 0/14, realignment 0/41 addressable, the confidence pin as a
  disqualification trap, and the realignment safety battery.
- **Generic mechanisms are judged on harm, validity, and cost — not on train-set gain.** A lever that adds
  nothing on train can still ship as insurance if it is provably harmless, and a lever that gains on train
  is still killed if it harms clean cases.
- **A line-level gap in our code is not the same as a point on the board.** Reading six competing MIT-licensed
  solutions found three real gaps in our matcher and parser. All three were built, and on train the system's
  existing redundancy already covered two of them completely: the honest yield was +0 train points from the
  fusion costs and the sponsor vote, and the entire measured gain came from the one mechanism (clipped-flag
  recovery) that reached a field nothing else could read. Their value is private-set coverage, and we say so
  rather than dressing the diff up as a score.
- **A synthetic census is a hypothesis about precision, not a measurement of it.** The margin layer's own
  synthetic census forecast a cost of 0.6 correct fills and the corpus charged 8. The same instrument, run
  instead over the *real* fill population, is what set the generator-inversion reader's margin correctly and
  what killed the cross-channel veto outright. Census on real slots, then A/B in composition — the layer
  that looks safest in isolation can be the one that over-rejects once the other layers are present.
- **Answer-key and injection channels are anti-signal** and are never read; hidden spans are stripped before
  rasterization.
- **Scoring runs need a quiet machine.** The per-case wall-clock deadline means concurrent load manufactures
  fallback rows and depresses the score, so no two full-train scoring runs ever overlapped.
